# Flying to Cavite
Last June, I flew to Manila and have a trip arranged to Cavite for my internship/OJT at Analog Devices, General Trias, Cavite. Among my batch in Computer Engineering, I was the only one who had to leave the Negros island for OJT. I am not sure about the other engineering programs if they had students moved places for OJT too. I can say it was not the usual OJT most of the students from my department had. Not trying to be egocentric here, but I mean in terms of moving out of your hometown, with little to no safety net, has to live alone, live in an unfamiliar environment,  and handle corporate standards for a merely 240-hour OJT can be overwhelming for most—even for me. Moving out sounds reasonable for a more lasting employment, but it could be not worth the hassle for a 240-hour OJT. Despite that, I am still grateful I left my comfort zone and actually flew there anyway. It was a great opportunity to have an internship in an American semiconductor company! I get to have semiconductor/corporate experience as a student, meet brilliant engineers, and meet fellow thriving students across the country. On personal character growth, I realized how fast money runs out, how much I have taken for granted living with parents, and how to take accountability.

I am lucky to have siblings in Metro Manila—I had two of my siblings and my brother's former classmate help me find a bedspace to rent in Javalera, Gen. Trias. Which made my struggle when I got there more bearable.

I still got to visit my siblings in Metro Manila before my internship ends and going back to Negros.

## Demanding bootcamp
The bootcamp lasted for ~240 hours. The bootcamp covers the process of verifying an IC before being taped-out into an actual silicon. You can imagine my experience as a bootcamp participant like I am still a student attending my classes and answering assignments and activities but in a corporate office setting instead of classrooms. The engineers and my colleagues agree that the phase of the bootcamp was indeed fast. If you ask me how fast-phased it was, imagine a whole semester of your major course squashed into 5 weeks. Despite that, I still manage to pass with a relatively high grade 😅. Thanks to the considerate engineers, fellow bootcamp colleagues,  and my instructor (which also happens to speak Hiligaynon) who made my stay there enjoyable despite the challenging nature of the bootcamp.

I was able to make new friends as well during the bootcamp. Some of them were from top universities in the Philippines!

## The mischievous tenant
Let me share some of my absent-minded moments while living alone for a month. I was the  *pasaway* (stubborn) young boy to my landlady. Not because I was naughty but I was just... let's just say *inexperienced*.

- On the second day of my stay at the bedspace, there was a water interruption in some parts of General Trias. I woke up with no water coming out from the tap so I took a bath with the drinking water from the drinking water container. I left and went to work not knowing I have left the faucet open. When I got home, I noticed there were puddles everywhere. At first, I thought it came from a leak somewhere. Then I realized, the water bucket inside the bathroom which was empty in the morning when I left is full. So I asked the landlady what happened, she told me "*Naiwan mong bukas 'yong gripo*" (I had unintentionally left the faucet open). Obviously I had to scoop the water into the bathroom and mop it myself. My mom usually does the housekeeping at home (thanks ma!).
- I had a presentation during the last day of the bootcamp proper. That day, I was not even able to sleep much.  I had to expedite doing my slide deck presentation. In the morning, I ironed my white t shirt and left early 'cause I still had to finalize my presentation and study it. I forgot to plug off the iron. I went back home in the afternoon and slept 'cause finally I can rest well after the rigorous bootcamp. I woke up at 8 then I saw the iron still plugged in. Thanks to the engineers who designed that iron that it did not explode and started a fire 😬.
- The lock of the bathroom door that can be seen on the right side of the photo is just one nail tied to the door which you can insert in a hole outside of the bathroom. The bathroom window was just adjacent to the door. Due to heavy wind, the force of the wind that enters the windows bangs the door making it sound so loud to other tenants. The landlady texted me about the complaint. Even when I lock the door, the wind was just so strong that it loosened the nail out of the hole.
- Casually forget the key inside of the room.

Living alone really does introduce me to the mindset of what being independent is—it made me aware of the gaps when there is someone looking after you vs doing everything by yourself. I made some videos talking to myself when I was alone in there for posterity. I'd like to share it but I would rather keep it to myself and cringe about it years later—maybe I'd share it with others, who knows?

There were nights that I had toothache and I had no one to talk about it. There were nights that the bootcamp felt so overwhelming that I just want to get it done ASAP and go back to Bacolod. It showed me what being scared and unsure but showing up anyway looks like.

## My *probinsyano* moment in EDSA
*Probinsyano* means country lad or a man/boy who grew up in rural area of the country. Strictly speaking, I am not considered as one as I was raised and born in an urban area. However, not the same scale as Metro Manila.

After my last day of my internship, it was Saturday and I decided to go to Metro Manila to stay at my sisters place for a while before going home. I also informed the HR to have me fetched on Monday from the address I gave them to NAIA. I took a bus from Gen. Trias Cavite to [PITX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para%C3%B1aque_Integrated_Terminal_Exchange). My sister instructed me to not book a ride-hailing service from PITX 'cause it would cost me more. Instead, she told me to take a bus from PITX and then drop off at Robinsons Galleria and then book Grab there. So I took another bus from PITX. I do not remember what was the exact route of that bus but I think it was the EDSA Carousel. 

For context, I had a ~15kg luggage with me plus 2 other full bags that had my pillow, utensils, and other necessities when I was staying in Cavite. The ride was not very comfortable obviously, it looked like I had my entire house on my hands. The bus stopped at MOA, SM Megamall, and One Ayala. Of course I assumed it would stop in Robinsons Galleria as well—it didn't. I just randomly dropped off somewhere in Cubao then I realized I am in the middle of the road. Well, not exactly, it is like the bus stop is in the middle of 2 lanes.... look at the bus stop with the "Main Avenue" signage. That is where I dropped off carrying everything but the kitchen sink.

## Thesis & GECs
"We" here refers to me and my thesis partner, Joross. Yes, our thesis is only by pair. Quite expensive, yes. Good thing for us is our thesis focuses on architectural system of computing. It is **architecture-oriented** (E.g., Hardware acceleration and designing a CPU) so we don't have to collect survey responses from people or do carpentry/farming which in my opinion takes so much time and is logistically more challenging.

I believe the common and most expected thesis for undergrad, application-oriented (E.g., Automated plant watering system and Smart bin segregation system), albeit genuinely impressive and useful, in my opinion is more expensive, physically and logistically harder to implement in a short period of time.

In my senior year, I still have several General Education Courses (colloquially minor subjects) to take, aside from my thesis as my junior year (3rd year) was loaded with major engineering courses prerequisites, setting aside GECs (except for religious education, which we have to take once every year). As of this writing, we already have a working demo and we are making a steady progress. Our final defense is on November, a week after our educational tour in Malaysia. We aim to have our prototype be finished before October ends. I already anticipate and imagine ourselves spending our nights writing thesis paper during our stay in Malaysia.

We occasionally do our thesis in a coffee/vape shop somewhere in [Villa Angela Marketplace](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Sga7NpHz4SAwMfG98), beside Bacolod City Hall. We don't vape, it is just the only place I can find where there is reliable internet and there are no many people around so we are less distracted. Idk if it is weird when I tell people I go to vape shop to do my thesis, they act jokingly surprised or confused. Maybe vape shop is not just the first place that comes to mind when we talk about doing something that relates to schooling. We can do it in the university, but the laboratories and the other available spots are not always available plus the distribution of the internet within the university is not always reliable.

## Learning how to ride a bike
At this age, I just learned how to ride a bike. Ruejen, a classmate of mine, occasionally uses his mountain bike to commute to school if the weather permits. I asked him if he could give me lessons some time, on the first day, I spent 3 hours just to practice balancing. I felt like a Exhilarated child when I pedalled a bike for the first time! I practiced in an empty parking lot inside the university, it was exam week and our schedule was empty that day. The next time, Ruejen came along with us 'cause I asked him to solder my Arduino nano as Joross and I work with our thesis. After working, I practiced biking again in the City Halls playground, which I can finally pedal and balance more comfortably. I still struggle a bit with starting and stopping, but it is something I can improve over time through practice. I do not have my own bike so it has been a while but at least the hardest part is over. The next time I ride a bike it would feel more instinctive as my brain can already imagine what it feels like to balance a machine.

This was not the first time I practiced a bike though. I remember when I was a child, my neighbors taught me but it never clicked. When I was in Metro Manila, my sister rented a bike for an hour when we were in Quezon City Circle park, but the bike was not beginner-friendly. It was a fixed-gear bamboo bike, it was heavy and heavy to pedal. Making it harder to balance, let alone propel the bike.

If you don't know how to ride a bike, Ruejen recommends learning with a geared and lightweight bike. Start with lower gears as it they're lighter to spin the pedal and you can also coast (freewheel) making it easier to learn balance.

## Doing impromptu speech
During our Engineering days in the University, our org president struggled to find a representative for the English and Filipino impromptu. She persuaded me because nobody else had the guts to join. Maybe I was the safest fallback she could find as nobody else from the entire **ICpEP.SE UNOR Chapter** was willing to participate, just to avoid getting disqualified. In short, I was just pushed to do it. She even offered to buy me Jollibee C1 just to make me say yes... I eventually did just for the org, and for the C1 as well... I mean... I can buy C1 myself but I also told myself this is my last undergrad year anyway so might as well try.

The thing is, it is not really on my personality to participate in literary events like this. Maybe I am just fond of figuring out unfamiliar systems despite the consequences—provided that it will grow something in my character. I consider myself optimistic and introverted. Despite the possibility of being petrified on stage while having hundreds of people stare at me when I genuinely do not know what to say, I told myself "I went to Cavite alone and unsure. Lived alone and performed well regardless. I have survived awkward situations, rejections personally and professionally, and even worse than that. A 3-minute impromptu speaking, even at worst case scenario is nothing compared to what I have already gone through".

Okay, maybe that sounds a bit dramatic, partly it was just main character energy. Some people may have faced worse adversities. But the point I am trying to make here is I know that even if it does not go well, I'll survive it anyway.

I remember during the English impromptu, while the contestant before me was speaking and I was thinking about the structure of my own answer as I read my prompt. I genuinely almost blanked out while thinking. Like I had the thought of just running away before it was my turn. The prompt that was given to me goes something like what can students do to fight climate change. I did not do well in the English impromptu, I admit. For the Filipino, I did a little bit better—at least, but my time ran out. The prompt for the Filipino went like how should the Philippines handle the tension in West Philippine Sea.

Frankly speaking though, partly I also have shortcomings to myself. I was not able to prepare and practice enough the days before the said event. The main reason I agreed really is I wanted to hone my public speaking skills. I had public speaking experience before but it was not impromptu nor a competition. I wanted to stress-test this aspect of me.
